DEVELOPER MASTER REQUIREMENTS | VERSION 2.0

Independent NFR-first rodeo editorial website + custom publishing CMS + SEO Intelligence

Prepared: 28 October 2026 | Status: Approved functional architecture; visual direction pending final sign-off

Purpose: A buildable specification for a developer or development team. No code is authorized by this document alone.

How to use this document

  1. Review Sections 1–5 for scope, information architecture and page behavior.

  2. Implement Section 6 as the editorial CMS contract and Section 7 as the data-model contract.

  3. Implement Sections 8–11 for SEO, security, performance and operational reliability.

  4. Use Sections 12–14 as delivery phases, acceptance gates and open decision log.

  5. Raise any conflicting or unspecified requirement before implementation; do not silently invent event facts or publishing policies.

1. Product mission and non-negotiable rules

NFRGuide is an independent information and editorial publication covering the National Finals Rodeo (NFR) and wider rodeo world. It helps fans find reliable schedules, competition explanations, results coverage, legitimate viewing information, tickets and travel guidance, entertainment and other fan experiences.

  • Independent publisher: no representation as an official NFR, PRCA, broadcaster, ticketing provider or event organizer. Include clear independent-site identity and editorial/about/disclosure pages.

  • Article-first: articles are the primary editorial unit. Competitor profiles, results, schedules and news are articles with optional structured relationships—not a live statistics platform.

  • Original, sourced, accurate and useful content. Time-sensitive dates, viewing rights, ticket availability and prices require verification and review dates.

  • No unauthorized stream hosting or embedded pirated feeds; direct users to legitimate sources.

  • No fake statistics, SEO scores, event dates, countdowns, audience counts or search engine indexing claims.

  • AdSense and affiliate monetization must be transparent, clearly disclosed and subordinate to the reader experience.

  • Custom admin/CMS, not WordPress or Sanity; existing SportingSpy approach is a reference, not a shared brand or database.

  1. Site header and five-section navigation; prominent search.

  2. Lead cinematic editorial hero with headline, description and article link; no invented claims.

  3. Latest NFR coverage (automatic feed with manual editorial overrides).

  4. NFR quick-access row: schedules, results, disciplines, how to watch, tickets.

Homepage sections are controlled from admin using approved section types, manual curation and/or filtered feeds. Provide preview and publish controls; avoid an unrestricted page builder.

3.2 Article page behavior

  • One H1, optional secondary editorial deck, distinct excerpt, author, published/updated/reviewed dates as appropriate.

  • Featured image with rights/credit and contextual ALT text. Rich text with semantic headings, lists, quotes, tables, accessible links and responsive images.

  • For relevant guides, concise answer near top; optional auto-generated table of contents for longer articles.

  • Clear citations/sources and commercial disclosures; related articles and contextual links.

  • Article type changes validation and optional components, not the core article storage or editor.

  • Draft preview must be authenticated or securely tokenized, noindex and never exposed through public search/sitemaps.

4.1 Responsive behavior

  • Mobile-first layouts; practical viewport checks at ~360, 390, 768, 1024 and 1440 px, plus fluid resizing.

  • Desktop: horizontal nav, wide hero, multi-column cards; optional article sidebar/table of contents.

  • Tablet: reduced columns and simplified header as needed.

  • Mobile: menu drawer, single-column article, 1–2-column cards where readable, no horizontal overflow.

  • Touch targets, contrast, keyboard access, skip navigation, semantic landmarks, focus and reduced-motion preferences.

  • Reserve ad/image dimensions to reduce layout shifts; never put intrusive ads between essential answer and source context.

5.1 Admin interaction standards

  • Persistent sidebar/topbar, breadcrumbs, global search, New Article, notifications and environment label.

  • Server-side pagination, sorting, filters and permission-safe bulk actions on lists.

  • All forms: explicit required markers, inline validation, save feedback, loading/error/empty states.

  • Destructive actions require confirmation and are logged.

  • Desktop-first admin with usable mobile workflows; no reliance on hover-only interactions.

6. Editorial editor and workflow

6.1 Required SportingSpy-aligned field order

  1. Main Headline (required to publish).

  2. Secondary Editorial Deck (optional; one-line subheading).

  3. Editorial Excerpt / Summary (required for published article cards/previews).

  4. Article Body (validated structured rich-text JSON).

  5. SEO Title (independently editable; length advisory, not a rigid universal cutoff).

  6. Meta Description (independently editable; may be suggested from excerpt).

  7. Image SEO Title.

  8. Image ALT Text (contextual, editable per placement).

Additional editor panels: article type, primary category, tags, optional hub/event/edition/discipline associations, featured media, image credit/rights, sources, author/reviewer, canonical/robots, affiliate/sponsor disclosure, publication schedule, revisions and audit history.

6.2 Configurable article types

Seed types: News; Event Update; Event Guide; Schedule; Results & Standings; How to Watch; Preview; Competitor Profile; Rules & Format; History; Records & Past Winners; Entertainment Guide; Ticket Guide; Venue Guide; Travel Guide; Product Buying Guide; Product Review; General Information. Types are configurable through admin and share one editor; type-specific SEO profiles and conditional validation.

6.3 Workflow

Draft → In Review → Approved/Scheduled → Published → Archived, with preview, unpublish, corrections, review dates and revision restoration. “Approved” can be represented as a review state rather than a public content status. Solo Super Admin may move through stages without separate users, but authorization is still enforced.

  1. Create draft; autosave and record revisions.

  2. Assign classification and optional rodeo relationships; attach sources and media.

  3. Run field, integrity, rights, disclosure and SEO checks.

  4. Submit for review or self-approve if authorized.

  5. Preview exact public rendering.

  6. Publish immediately or schedule with explicit timezone.

  7. At scheduled execution, revalidate; on failure leave unpublished and notify/log.

  8. On updates, maintain dates, revisions, canonical paths and redirects.

6.4 Publishing blockers versus advice

Confirmed security, legal/compliance, ownership/rights, required fields, invalid URL/relationship and critical technical failures block publication. Routine SEO suggestions warn but do not block. Uncertain automated factual checks request human review rather than asserting a claim is false. Suppression of confirmed blockers is not available to ordinary editors.

7. Logical database schema contract

PostgreSQL relational design; UUID IDs for most entities; timestamps in UTC with display timezone; explicit foreign keys, unique constraints, indexes and deletion rules. Prisma is the preferred ORM but executable models, SQL constraints and migrations remain developer deliverables. The table inventory is logical and may expand for auth/ownership infrastructure.

7.3 Database integrity requirements

  • One stable event record may link to many hubs and editions; do not duplicate events for each hub.

  • Edition uniqueness is (event_id, edition_slug), not merely (event_id, year). Some events may have multiple occurrences per year.

  • All article associations use junction tables with composite unique keys.

  • Protect referenced entities from destructive deletion; archive or soft-delete where appropriate.

  • Normalize and reserve public URLs transactionally; ensure redirect generation when published slugs change.

  • No unchecked polymorphic owner references: SEO metadata/findings and URL registry require explicit FK ownership or a validated ownership registry.

  • Enforce data constraints in DB where feasible and repeat business validations in application services.

  • Do not store arrays of relational IDs in JSON when a proper junction table is available.

7.4 Deliverables required from developer

  • Complete Prisma schema, enum definitions, relations, indexes, unique constraints and SQL migrations.

  • Physical ERD including junction tables, SEO ownership, sessions and job infrastructure.

  • Seed data for five primary sections, seven NFR disciplines, article types, roles and SEO profiles.

  • Migration plan, rollback strategy, backup/restore test and automated referential-integrity tests.

8. SEO Intelligence specification

One configurable rule engine shared by editor checks and scheduled audits. Do not create separate competing SEO engines. Checks must be explainable and rule-scoped, with persisted scan and finding history.

12 groups Expected checks
1. On-page SEO title, meta description, headings, primary topic clarity
2. Search appearance Duplicate titles/descriptions, snippet readiness
3. Technical Canonicals, noindex, URL collisions, sitemap eligibility
4. Content/topic Completeness, readability, useful answers, factual context
5. External links Official source verification, link health, sponsored classification
6. Internal links Orphan content, broken references, contextual linking
7. Image SEO ALT, credit, image rights, optimization
8. Structured data Article/NewsArticle, CollectionPage, SportsEvent only when warranted
9. AI search readiness Direct answers, source clarity, entity context
10. Freshness Review dates, stale event/viewing/ticket information
11. Event SEO Hub/event/edition consistency and lifecycle
12. Redirects Loops, chains, invalid targets and status codes

8.1 SEO data and lifecycle

  • seo_profiles configure checks by article type; seo_rules store code, severity, enabled state, version and thresholds; seo_rule_scopes override applicability.

  • seo_scans track scope, status, engine version, timestamps and initiator.

  • seo_findings use stable fingerprint, severity, owner, status (open/resolved/reopened/suppressed where permitted), first/last detection.

  • seo_scan_findings record evidence from each scan; seo_finding_events record all status changes and reasons.

  • Editors can navigate from issue to affected content/field. Only authorized roles can suppress non-blocking findings, with reason and optional expiry.

  • No fabricated SEO score, ranking, indexing or analytics. Connected Search Console/Bing data must be labeled as actual verified integration data.

  • Expensive full-site scans and link checks execute asynchronously, not on public page requests.

8.2 Metadata, indexing and link policy

  • Canonical, robots and sitemap eligibility must derive from one central policy. Draft/unpublished/private/noindex pages are excluded from public XML sitemaps.

  • Normal editorial source links are followable by default; internal links use normal anchor markup.

  • Affiliate/paid links use rel="sponsored" and visible disclosure. Untrusted links may use nofollow; UGC uses ugc if introduced.

  • Use JSON-LD types that reflect actual content. An article about an event is not automatically a SportsEvent page.

  • Redirects must avoid loops/chains; use 301/302/307/308 as appropriate and validate destination safety.

9. Search, monetization and content operations

9.1 Internal search

Launch with PostgreSQL full-text search over published, eligible articles, hubs, events and editions. Provide content-type filters, pagination, relevant ranking and useful zero-results guidance. Record aggregate normalized search queries and result counts with a retention policy; do not collect unnecessary personal data.

9.2 Commercial controls

Commercial link registry records destination, partner, relationship type, status and optional tracking configuration. Editorial link insertion distinguishes normal, sponsored, untrusted and future UGC links. Visible disclosures are mandatory where appropriate. Ad slots must be labeled, reserved in layout, policy-compliant and not mistaken for editorial recommendations.

9.3 Media and sources

Use object storage for media, with safe upload validation, resizing, credit/rights fields and contextual ALT text. Keep a reusable source directory and article-source verification notes. Time-sensitive viewing/ticket information must have suitable references and review dates.

10. Security, authorization and operations

  • Use vetted authentication libraries; secure password hashing, session rotation, MFA for privileged roles, rate limits and secure reset/recovery flows.

  • Role-based permissions: Super Admin, Administrator, Editor, Author and SEO Manager. Enforce on server and APIs, not only sidebar visibility.

  • Audit create/edit/publish/unpublish/permission/SEO rule/redirect/commercial changes.

  • Protect against XSS, CSRF, injection, SSRF through URL fetches, unsafe uploads and open redirects; validate rich-text nodes and external URLs.

  • Persistent background job queue for scheduled publishing, SEO scans, sitemap updates and link checks; retries, idempotency, deduplication, failure monitoring.

  • Backups, restore testing, error monitoring, log retention, secrets management and environment separation (development/staging/production).

  • Respect copyright and privacy; no unauthorized live-stream content or copyrighted media without appropriate rights.

10.1 Recommended stack (subject to technical sign-off)

Next.js (App Router), React, TypeScript, PostgreSQL, Prisma, Tailwind CSS, Tiptap/ProseMirror, object storage, a durable worker/queue, Vitest and Playwright. Deployment vendor, auth library, cache, queue provider and analytics integrations require a documented implementation decision before development. Avoid hard-coding infrastructure assumptions.

11. Performance, accessibility and SEO delivery

  • Indexable public content should use server-side rendering or static generation with appropriate revalidation.

  • Responsive image optimization, lazy loading below the fold, caching, pagination and minimal client-side JavaScript.

  • Target good Core Web Vitals at p75: LCP <= 2.5 s, INP <= 200 ms, CLS <= 0.1; measure rather than guarantee.

  • Target WCAG 2.2 AA for key user journeys: keyboard access, focus, contrast, labels, landmarks, skip links and reduced motion.

  • Robots, XML sitemaps, canonical tags, redirects, structured data and preview protections must be validated in staging.

  • Use verified event data; do not manufacture dates, tickets, prices, streaming availability, athlete results or countdowns.
